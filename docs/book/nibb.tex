\chapter*{Preface}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Preface}

This book is written for one reader at three moments: the person who wants
to understand what \nibb{} is trying to be, the person who has to change its
code, and the person sitting at the prompt. It is organised accordingly.

\textbf{Part I} states the idea: why a coding agent's secrets should be
\emph{credentials} the harness never sees rather than \emph{content} it tries
to recognise; why one guard at one boundary is worth more than many checks
in many places; why the log, not the model's context, is the truth.
\textbf{Part II} is the plan: a hand-written bootstrap, then a harness that
builds itself, phase by phase, each phase retiring one of the temporary
protections the bootstrap runs under. \textbf{Part III} is an inventory of
what exists as of \code{v0.1-bootstrap}. \textbf{Part IV} walks the code: one
tool call followed through eleven files, with the actual source. \textbf{Part V}
is a tutorial and reference for \nibb{} Scheme, the small language in which
\nibb's own tools are written. \textbf{Part VI} is the operator's manual.

Everything in this book is derived from the repository: \file{SPEC.md},
\file{plan.md}, \file{docs/scheme.md}, \file{docs/adr/}, and the source.
Where the book and the repository disagree, the repository is right and the
book has a bug.

\paragraph{Status.} The code described is tag \code{v0.1-bootstrap}
(2026-09-21) plus the standing-approval amendment committed the same day.
Phase~1b (the full v0 kernel) was in review at press time; its forms are
described in Part~V with that caveat.
